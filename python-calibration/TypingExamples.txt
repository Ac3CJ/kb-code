The apostrophe (') wears many hats. It shows possession (Sarah's book), forms contractions (don't, can't), and even indicates the plural of letters (mind your p's and q's). While its functions may seem diverse, they all share a common thread: making written language more efficient and expressive.

Test 2
Quotation marks do exactly what their name suggests -- they quote! They enclose the exact words someone spoke or wrote, ensuring accuracy and attribution. Whether it's dialogue in a novel, a quote in a research paper, or a snippet of song lyrics, quotation marks give credit where it's due and add authenticity to your writing.

Test 3
The question mark (?) is the punctuation of inquiry. It turns statements into questions, inviting responses and sparking curiosity. Whether it's a simple 'How are you?' or a complex philosophical question, the question mark signals a desire for information or a deeper understanding.
